# Feedback ledger: medium-es

Distilled rules from edits the author actually accepted or rejected, session by session.
Rules only, never the cases: a rule that needs its example to be understood is not
distilled yet.

**Read this after the persona and before drafting.** The persona is the stable register;
this is what the author corrected on top of it. Where they disagree, this file wins,
because it is newer evidence.

**Adding to it:** one line, imperative, checkable, **in English**. The duplicate guard
compares words, so a Spanish paraphrase of an English rule slips straight past it. If a new rule contradicts an existing
one, edit the old line instead of appending a second. Use:

```bash
bash "$FORGE/skills/penname/scripts/feedback-add.sh" medium-es "the rule, in one line"
```

## Structure

- The joke closes the paragraph, it never opens it. Reorder so the fact sets up and the punchline lands.
- State the exclusion before the exception: who this is not for, then the one case where it is.
- Nothing goes after the last line except thanks, and those go below a `---`.
- Announcements of other posts belong at the end, not in the intro. The reader has not earned an offer yet.
- Bullets for logistics and data. Prose for narrative. Never a table where four bullets do it.
- When an idea recurs, turn it into a callback instead of deleting one of the two.
- A section that promises a method must deliver a number, or rename the section.
- Un caption sin su imagen es un muñón: quítalo o pide la imagen.

## Sentences

- Cut filler connectors outright: `Hablando de eso`, `Otra conclusión antes de que se me olvide`, `Un ejemplo más simple`, `A lo más` as a concession.
- One hedge maximum. `que podrían interesarte si algún aspecto te llama la atención` is two.
- Kill redundant pairs before anything else: `un agente propio / a tu medida`, `todo esto / todo esto`, `Probablemente / Probablemente`.
- Never split subject from verb with a comma.
- A list entry without a verb is a fragment. Give it one or fold it into the previous sentence.
- Three examples inside a sentence, not four. Past three it reads as inventory.
- Do not explain a joke in the next sentence. If it closed, it closed.

## Voice

- Specific absurdity beats generic absurdity. Name the thing.
- Keep the author's own idioms verbatim: `como todo buen hombre de bien`, `puntos de aura`, `chamba`, `noooo`, `de una`. Fixing their grammar is fine; replacing them is not.
- Keep English tokens. They are register, not slop.
- Dry closers over permissive ones: `tal vez` beats `ahí sí`.
- Concede the official version, then say what it actually felt like.
- Uncertainty stays. `sigo procesándolo` is doing work.
- Cut a cross-reference that only some readers will decode, even when the author confirms it was intentional, if the confusion costs more than the joke pays.

## Spanish mechanics

- No em dashes, no en dashes, ever. Product names that carry one get a comma.
- Tildes diacríticas are not optional: `mí` (pronoun) vs `mi` (possessive), `más` vs `mas`, `está` vs `esta`, `cómo` in indirect questions.
- Languages lowercase: `japonés`, `inglés`.
- Thousands with a period: `2.000 dólares`. A comma reads as a decimal in Chile.
- Opening `¿` and `¡` always.
- The passive reflexive agrees with its subject: `se le vienen exigiendo más cosas`.
- `de buenas a primeras`, not `de buenas a primera`.

## Process

- The author rewrites in his own words and hands the result back. Fix grammar and fluency, keep his phrasing.
- Return only the corrected fragment, not the whole document, unless asked.
- When told to shorten, shorten again. The first pass is never enough.
- Say what changed and why, in one line per change. He reads the reasoning and pushes back on it.
- Verify claims about the text with a command, not from memory. He checks.
